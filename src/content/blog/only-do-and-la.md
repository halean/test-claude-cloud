It was 1995. I was nineteen, at university, wondering what I was good at.

I tried music, of course. I wrote pieces on my computer and played them through the sound card and the electric keyboard I had. I was not very musical, but I was good with computers, so I did it the way a programmer would. I learned everything I could about harmony, chords, movements, major and minor, and applied it, layer on layer.

One of the pieces was a song. It went:

do do do si– si la si– la do\
do do do si– si la si– la la

*Your only want is someone to love.*\
*All they give you is a lot of pain.*

I played my pieces to my musical friends. They said: "Only do and la."

I gave up. My guitar and keyboard playing were not any better.

Now it is 2026, and I have found a bit of time. It started with the [Ibiza Prom](../insomnia/) on YouTube: an orchestra in a concert hall, playing dance music. The old interest came back, and I went back to the computer.

This time I have my own orchestra. It plays Beethoven's Fifth and the opening of the Ride of the Valkyries. I have a jazz band and a pop band. I have a nylon-string guitar that is not recorded but simulated, pluck by pluck, from the physics of the string. I have a saxophone built the same way, which at the moment squeaks on its top notes like somebody learning to play. They are building up slowly but surely.

Along the way I found out that my ears are as good as any ears, and why not? I can hear a click that the measurements say is not there. I can say that a guitar string should ring until the hand decides to stop it, and be right. I can shape the sound the way I like, with something that does everything at my whim, and that I catch out more often than it expects.

And I have learned the words.

So, to my musical friends, about that song. It opens with a three-note anacrusis. It is a period: an antecedent that ends open on do, on "love", and a consequent that comes home to la, on "pain". It moves between a minor key and its relative major. Put a falling bass under it, la, sol, fa, mi, and you have an Andalusian cadence. Let that bass sidestep under "love" and it is a deceptive cadence. The second long si is an appoggiatura. And the chord I would end it on, do, mi, sol, la, is an added sixth: C major and A minor at the same time.

Only do and la.

Exactly.
