Bangkok 2026. We stopped here for 24 hours on the way to Sài Gòn. It was partly for me
to uncompress, for my son to revisit Bangkok. We checked in the hotel, had a rest, then
got down to the river side. The smell of the river, according to the son, “just like
Vietnam”, before we saw the river. Some consultation with Claude suggested a ferry trip.
We did it, gladly, from River City to IconSiam. IconSiam was such a new concept that
suited Thai newly minted middle class so well. Six floors of restaurants, a whole hall
of air conditioned street food, one could not expect more. And the food was affordable,
like everything else in Bangkok, from public transport, upscale hotels, tuktuk rides,
coconut water. And the food was well made, unsurprisingly given how Thai people like to
eat out. The Thai milk tea might shock some with the amount of condensed milk and
concentrated black tea used, not me though. Then a ferry back to River City, a tuktuk
ride to Chinatown, to see the real street food. We were not too adventurous, so just
walked along the Yaowarat admiring people eating local delicacies. It was lively, unlike
the Chinatown in Kuala Lumpur where everyone seemed to believe the good days were over.
A ride of tuktuk back to the hotel, again, affordable.

First night in Bangkok impression: it was lively, not because of the tourists, but
because it is. The big shopping mall reflected the difference from Kuala Lumpur: much
space dedicated to food, and food was more palatable to our tongues. The flight to
Bangkok was weird though: the demography was different: lot of single middle-aged white
men, unlike flights to Singapore, Hồ Chí Minh City, Kuala Lumpur, whose flyers are mostly
families and backpackers.
