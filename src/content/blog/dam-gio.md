_I read an article: “Tuần trước, tôi theo chân chị về miền Tây ăn giỗ.” Loud karaoke
speakers, beer opened one after another, pressure to drink, a fight between two groups of
young men. “Cuộc vui lấn át ý nghĩa tưởng niệm”, the author concluded. I read it and it
feels so AI written. It does not have a real sense of being there at all._

Like “ăn cưới”, “đám cưới”, “ăn giỗ” always has two parts: the ceremony, and the banquet.
The banquet is always a banquet, with drunkards and the likes. They even have different
mâm: mâm cho chú bác, mâm trẻ em, mâm thanh niên. Even when I was young, 40 years ago, ăn
giỗ was like that, minus the yet to be invented karaoke speakers.

The đám giỗ of my childhood were like this: close elder relatives always turned up early
with their own dishes, placed on the altar, the incense lighted. Then they talked, not
necessarily about the one on the altar, being dead a long time ago. Then younger
relatives turned up, lighted some candle to one they never met, then sat down and ate and
drank. My family always debated about whether to invite a drunkard relative. My father
did not want to, my grandmother said otherwise: the giỗ was for my grandfather. We always
ended up inviting him. The feasts were always with alcohol, beers mostly, at the time
when beers were still a treat. Some would come and go early, some would stay late and
drink a lot, the drunkard caused some problems this year but not that. The pressure to
drink was moderate, and there was always a no drink table, or some elderly you suddenly
wanted to talk to.

The real remembrance happened the night before, with the early guests who came and went
already. Even in the city, the preparation was the fun part. In the past, intricate
preparation to overcome scarcity; now, elaborate preparation is the only meaningful
offering. The labour is the offering. These days, people would pick a weekend, so that
the city dwellers can join, and combine several ancestors together. I bet the giỗ he
attended was just like that. A simple question would have unlocked so much: do you
always sing at giỗ? Why?

“Người nhặt rau, người kho cá”, the article said. Nobody does that. Cá kho is an everyday
dish; you just don't serve cá kho at a banquet. They are making sure the deep fried fish
are crunchy and standing up. I would be very offended if my banquet preparation was
described as nhặt rau, kho cá.
