Working with AIs is very much like going to the [gym](../phong-ta-1992/).

The ideology is the same. We don't need that much software engineering, form filling, receipt reading, contract preparing or court case arguing power, just as we no longer need muscle power to lift things, to run from A to B, or to jump over a dike while being chased by a sabre-toothed tiger. But we enjoy going to the gym to work our muscles, and we will enjoy sparring with AIs to work our brains.

The practice is similar as well. You build up your reps, small weights first. Give the AI simple problems, check its simple solutions, and train your brain to learn the way AIs work. Then work up to bigger, more complex problems.

Don't listen to the "simple prompt, a few hours later…" stories. If that were true, why wouldn't these people do simple prompts all day and all night? You do your reps. You learn your way up the scale of problems, and the strengths and weaknesses of AIs along the way.

Don't look at other people's reps or weights. They were either born with the ability or worked their way up there, and neither has anything to do with you.

More reps, more resistance, more gains. Every time.
